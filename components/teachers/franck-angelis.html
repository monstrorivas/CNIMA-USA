Franck Angélis

As a composer, Franck Angélis has written an enormous body of work, including about a hundred teaching pieces, six multi-movement cycles, some forty pieces in a romantic or virtuosic vein, and a whole shelf of music for accordion duos and ensembles. Students grow up on it, and professionals keep coming back to it. In France, Félicien Brut features Franck's Amalgame in his solo recital, and Domi Emorine recorded an entire album of his music with the Russian accordionist Roman Jbanov. Christine Rossi, Mélanie Brégant and Dimitri Bouclier play his work too, as do international stars like New Zealand's Grayson Masefield.

So what makes his music so irresistible to play? Composer Bernard Cavanna put it well. The first thing that hits you is the energy. Each moment has one strong idea, laid down in bold, almost self-contained blocks, and then another idea bursts in, often in sharp contrast. Cavanna compares this to Shostakovich, Debussy and Maurice Ohana, and notes that Franck's formidable virtuosity "never comes at the expense of the music's expressive impulse. In a word, this is great art."

Franck has won three first prizes in international composition competitions and taken top honors at various international accordion competitions. He's also an accomplished pianist.

In addition, he's a highly respected teacher and regularly guest teaches at CNIMA's workshops in France. We're thrilled to welcome him to New Orleans.
