Jacques Mornet

In August, 17-year-old Futa Mizutani won the Junior Classical category at the World Cup accordion competition in Kazakhstan. A few weeks later, he took second place out of 26 competitors at the PIF International Competition in Castelfidardo, Italy, the birthplace of the modern accordion. Futa lives in Japan and has studied with Jacques Mornet at CNIMA since he was 11, traveling to France twice a year for long stays and keeping up his lessons online in between. At that same competition in Castelfidardo, CNIMA's Trio Synapse took third place in chamber music.

Results like these are nothing new for Jacques Mornet. Remarkably, it all started in a grade-school classroom.

Born in Avignon, Jacques began his career as a schoolteacher in Le Thor, a small village in Provence. Music kept pulling at him, though. Every Thursday he turned his classroom into a makeshift music school, and before long it was drawing more young musicians than he had regular pupils. Local officials noticed his energy and asked him to create a large regional music school. As his students started winning national and then international competitions, Jacques left the classroom to run the school full time.

In 1995, with Nathalie Boucheix, he co-founded CNIMA. Three decades on, Jacques and CNIMA have one of the most impressive competition records anywhere, with more than 128 international first prizes and counting.

His reach is truly global. As Secretary General of the World Accordion Confederation (CMA), he's in constant demand as a judge at major international competitions. He also leads workshops and masterclasses around the world, and he has worked alongside many of the instrument's greatest players, teachers and composers. Along the way he has received the Silver Disc from Moscow's Gnessin Academy and the 2015 Gus Viseur Prize.

His pedagogy and force are essential to CNIMA's workshops.
